My DJ teacher is a language model

5 min read

Two weeks ago I decided to learn to DJ. I have a Pioneer DDJ-FLX4, no musical background, and no interest in waiting for a weekly lesson to tell me what to practice. So I did the thing I do at work: I treated it as a systems problem and asked AI to design the system.

The result is a 12-week curriculum built for my exact controller and my exact goal, a recorded 30-minute mix by the end of August. Month one builds the fundamentals: knowing the FLX4 blind, a repeatable 3-step mixing routine, and EQ mixing so transitions sound clean instead of clumsy. Month two is genre work, hip-hop and house and the switch between them. Month three is content: rehearsing, recording, posting, iterating.

Why not a human teacher?

Honestly? Feedback loops. A weekly lesson gives you one correction cycle per week. An AI coach gives you one per practice session: I describe what went wrong with a transition and get a targeted drill for tomorrow. It's not better than a great human teacher. It's better than no teacher, infinitely more available, and it never sighs when I ask the same question twice.

What's surprised me so far

The curriculum's best advice had nothing to do with music: build a system so you never freeze up. Every mix follows the same 3-step routine, so under pressure my hands have a default. That's the same reason checklists exist in aviation and diligence, and it works exactly as well at 122 BPM.

I also built the curriculum into a small tracker app, because of course I did. Progress bars are a controlled substance and I am not strong.

Report from week six coming when I get there. The mix lands by end of August, good or not.
