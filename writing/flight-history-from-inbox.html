I rebuilt my entire flight history from my inbox

5 min read

I fly a lot, and like most frequent flyers I had no actual record of it. The data existed, though, scattered across years of confirmation emails from Delta, United, JetBlue, and a few bookings I'd rather not discuss on Spirit. So one weekend I wrote a script to read them all.

The build

The Gmail API pulls anything that looks like a flight confirmation. Then the messy part: every airline formats its emails differently, and the same airline formats them differently across years. Parsing them was less "elegant regex" and more "archaeology with error handling." The final output is a clean spreadsheet: date, route, airline, aircraft where available.

What the data said

Seeing years of travel as one dataset rearranges your memory of it. Routes I thought of as one-off trips turned out to be patterns. The map the script produced, every route drawn as an arc, is genuinely one of my favorite things I've made, and it cost nothing but a weekend.

Your inbox is a database. Most people just never run a query on it.

Steal this idea

The general trick applies to more than flights: receipts, subscriptions, reservations. Anywhere a company emails you a structured confirmation, there's a personal dataset waiting. If you've ever wondered where your money or your weekends went, the answer is probably already in your inbox.
