Designing an app for exactly two users

6 min read

Every product lesson on the internet assumes scale. Growth loops, onboarding funnels, retention curves. I'm building an app whose total addressable market is two people who share a Netflix login, and it turns out almost none of the standard advice applies.

The app, working name Duet, is a shared home base for a couple: one calendar, shared notes and grocery lists, a watchlist you both add to, savings goals, habits, a milestone timeline of the relationship, a photo memory wall, and a running list of date ideas. Nothing in it is technically novel. What's novel is the constraint.

Two users changes everything

With two users, there is no "average user." Every design decision is a negotiation between exactly two sets of preferences, and both people will notice every choice. There's also no anonymity: every grocery item, every unchecked habit, every movie added to the watchlist has a name attached. The app is less a product and more a shared room, and you design a room differently than a stadium.

At scale you design for behavior. At two, you design for a relationship.

The features that earn their place

My rule is that every screen has to reduce a real recurring conversation: "what's on this weekend," "what do we need from the store," "what was that show we wanted to watch." The milestone timeline was almost cut as sentimental, until I realized it's the feature that makes the app feel like ours instead of another productivity tool. Sentiment, it turns out, is retention.

Building it with AI

The whole thing started as a written spec in plain English, then became a working React prototype over a few evenings with AI handling the syntax while I handled the decisions. That workflow keeps surprising me: the bottleneck is never the code anymore. It's knowing what the thing should do, which no model can decide for you.

Next step is turning the prototype into a real mobile app. I'll write about that leap when I've survived it.
